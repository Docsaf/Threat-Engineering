Cloud in 14 Days: Building an AI-Enhanced SOC as a First Security Hire (Azure spine, AWS and GCP in scope)

No-BS operator's path: learn only what the SOC build needs, in the order the build needs it · 2.5 hrs/day (90 learn + 60 hands-on in your tenant) · Every day ends with an artifact · By Dr. Safi Mojidi · Hacking the Workforce · shared in the spirit of knowledge, health, wealth, and security

The spine: Microsoft's SC-200 (Security Operations Analyst) learning path IS this curriculum's backbone: it covers Defender XDR, Defender for Cloud, and Sentinel, free on Microsoft Learn, and doubles as exam prep. Credential ladder: SC-200 (Azure-native, sit it when ready), CySA+ as the vendor-neutral baseline (exam by end of your committed exam window, the committed measure), SANS SEC504/GCIH if budget appears (ask your sponsor alongside the insurance question, roughly $8-9k). Your inherited triage thresholds slot into Day 8. Everything hands-on is read-only or in Cyber-owned scope until the RBAC section below is agreed.

Week 1 · Foundations, mapped to a real estate, not a sandbox

Day 1 (Wed Sep 30) · Tenant orientation + the first investigation. Learn: Defender XDR portal structure, device discovery vs onboarding (SC-200 module 1). Hands-on: work the live case: a first-login finding: discovered devices showing zero onboarded in the portal. Advanced hunting: DeviceInfo | summarize by DeviceName, OSPlatform, OnboardingStatus. Reconcile against your MDM enrollment counts. Determine: real unmanaged endpoints, device-group scoping on your role, or a tenant mismatch. Artifact: investigation note #1, filed as your SOC's first case record.
Day 2 (Thu Oct 1) · Entra ID and identity as the perimeter. Learn: Entra roles, PIM, Conditional Access basics. Hands-on: map who currently holds security roles in the tenant; draft the RBAC table below as a proposal. Artifact: current-state role export + gaps.
Day 3 (Fri Oct 2) · Defender for Endpoint operations. Learn: onboarding methods, device groups, alert/incident model, response actions. Hands-on: verify your own enrolled device appears; trace one alert end to end; confirm the compliance evidence exports your certification needs. Artifact: MDE runbook page 1.
Day 4 (Sat Oct 3) · Defender for Cloud + the AWS bridge. Learn: CSPM vs workload protection, multicloud connectors. Hands-on: read-only review of connector state; draft the precise change ticket for the AWS CloudFormation connector for your infrastructure team. Artifact: the ticket text + connector plan mapped to your cloud metrics.
Day 5 (Sun Oct 4, light) · KQL, the SOC's native tongue. Learn: KQL fundamentals (Must Learn KQL series / SC-200 hunting modules). Hands-on: five queries against DeviceInfo, AlertInfo, IdentityLogonEvents. Artifact: personal query pack v1.
Day 6 (Mon Oct 5, light) · CySA+ enrollment + reading. Register exam window (target inside your committed window), skim objectives, map which domains this build already teaches (most of 1-3). Artifact: study calendar to exam date.
Day 7 (Tue Oct 6) · Sentinel architecture + cost model. Learn: workspaces, data connectors, analytics rules, ingestion pricing. Hands-on: inventory current connectors and tables; pull 7-day ingestion by table (feeds your budget model's sizing line). Artifact: ingestion baseline note.

Week 2 · From console operator to SOC designer

Day 8 (Wed Oct 7) · Triage doctrine. Learn: incident triage models (SC-200 incident mgmt). Hands-on: write your triage thresholds v0.1: severity definitions, escalation clocks, inherited thresholds reconciled against your IR playbook tiers. Artifact: triage threshold sheet (the SOC's first standard).
Day 9 (Thu Oct 8) · Automation rules + playbooks. Learn: Sentinel automation rules, Logic Apps playbooks. Hands-on: design (not deploy) three: auto-enrich with TI, auto-assign by remediation lane (endpoint / cloud), auto-close known-benign. Artifact: playbook specs with HITL points marked.
Day 10 (Fri Oct 9) · AI in the SOC, honestly. Learn: Security Copilot capabilities and pricing (SCU model), agent patterns for triage/enrichment/reporting. Hands-on: write the register entries for the three SOC agents below, delegated authority stated. Artifact: agent specs into your AI agent register.
Day 11 (Sat Oct 10) · Threat intel wiring. Learn: Sentinel TI connectors, custom indicators in Defender. Hands-on: enable-plan for CISA KEV + AIS + abuse.ch (costed, ticketed, not clicked). Artifact: TI ingestion plan tied to your migration parity checks.
Day 12 (Sun Oct 11, light) · Reporting engines. Draft the two products below (daily + weekly) as templates; test-generate one daily report by hand from portal data. Artifact: both templates v0.1.
Day 13 (Mon Oct 12, light) · CySA+ domain study block. Focus: incident response + reporting domains (they mirror days 8-12). Artifact: practice-test score baseline.
Day 14 (Tue Oct 13) · Assemble the POC. Write the one-page SOC POC: scope, the three agents with HITL gates, RBAC model, reporting cadence, inputs checklist status, success measures, 30-day pilot ask. Artifact: POC doc to your executive sponsor, with the cyber-insurance services answer folded in. This is the deliverable the 14 days exist for.

The AI-enhanced SOC POC: agree the shape up front

AgentDoesHITL required (non-negotiable)
Triage agentEnriches new alerts (TI, asset owner, prior history), proposes severity + lane, drafts the ticketHuman confirms severity Sev 0-2; agent may auto-file Sev 3-4 informational only
Investigation agentRuns the standard KQL pack per incident type, assembles a timeline draftHuman approves scope before any query touching identity or client-named resources; all response actions human-executed
Reporting agentGenerates the daily threat report and drafts the weekly brief from case dataHuman review before send, always; "no rework from me" is the tuning target, not a reason to remove the gate

Tenets kept: agents read and draft, humans decide and act; every agent action logged to the case record; agents registered with delegated authority stated; nothing agent-built touches client environments.

Communication + reporting products (the operational contract)

Daily Threat Report (security's partners: IT Ops, GRC, Legal): yesterday's incident counts by severity/lane, notable cases in two lines each, TI items relevant to your sectors, agent-drafted, human-sent by 09:00 ET. Weekly Threat Brief + Incident Retro (whole org): the week's picture, one lesson, one metric trending toward risk terms your executive team can use; grows into the risk measure. Measure for both: no weeks missed, no rework needed from you. Feedback loops that must exist before "operational": IT Ops (endpoint context + heads-up channel), infrastructure liaisons (cloud/client context), GRC (evidence + training linkage), Legal (contract notification triggers), People (joiner/leaver feed), BD (early warning both directions).

Inputs checklist: what the SOC needs to be operational (Day 1 investigation seeds this)

Device inventory reconciled (MDM = Defender = HR system counts) · role/tenant scoping verified (the 13-device case answers this) · log sources connected with ingestion costed · triage thresholds signed (Day 8) · two-lane routing live · IR playbook SLAs · TI feeds (Day 11 plan) · RBAC table agreed (above) · reporting templates (Day 12) · liaison roster per delivery team · insurance services confirmed with your sponsor: breach coach, IR retainer/forensics panel, notification hotline: ask which are included before buying anything they already provide · agent register entries filed.

Tooling beyond the Microsoft stack, only as needed: Jira (case records, exists) · Logic Apps (playbooks, licensed) · Security Copilot (priced per SCU: pilot decision AFTER manual baseline, week 3+) · no new purchases inside the 14 days.
